24

Two Weeks of Delulu

For two whole weeks, I was living inside a dream I had built entirely by myself.

A Mumbai halt. Thirty minutes. That's all I needed. I spent days convincing myself it was possible — checked online, read every thread, calculated every minute like a case study.

I wore my black outfit. Your favourite one. Crazy black jeans, black shirt, the whole thing. I made sure I looked like someone worth waiting half a day for.

I remember standing there at that gate, thinking — any minute now, a princess is going to walk through that door.

My eyes were already looking for you before I even landed.

25

IndiGo Broke Me

I landed thirty minutes early. Thirty whole minutes ahead of schedule — which honestly felt like a sign.

The first thing I did was text you. "I've landed."

I tried everything. Asked every person I could find to just let me step out for ten minutes. That's it. Ten minutes.

IndiGo said no.

And just like that, two weeks of hoping dried up in about thirty seconds. I went stunned. Couldn't process it. All those plans, that outfit, those eyes — all of it, waiting for someone who was already waiting on the other side.

You had taken half a day off. You had dressed like an absolute princess on that video call. And I was standing there, on the wrong side of a gate, not sure I even deserved to pick up the phone.

I'm sorry for that one. I really am.

26

God Was Just Making Us More Curious

I think I understand it now. Or at least I'm telling myself I do.

God has a habit of doing the exact opposite of what you're hoping for — especially when it's something that actually matters.

Maybe that IndiGo gate existed for a reason. Maybe we weren't quite ready for that moment yet. Maybe the universe decided our first real meeting deserved more than a ten-minute window at an airport terminal.

So instead of giving us the hug, it made us wait. Made us more thirsty for it. Added a few more months of longing to the tab.

And honestly? The next time I come, we're going to want that one hug more than we've ever wanted anything.

God is just keeping the interest running. 🐣🌻

27

Drunk Calls & Silent Sleeps

1st August. You went to a party.

I saw the pictures.

My eyes — I don't know how else to say this — were shining. You were wearing that outfit, being that version of yourself that makes people stop and look twice, and I sat there quietly wishing I was the one taking care of you that night.

You came back home after one of the best nights you'd had in a while. Drunk. Happy. Glowing. And somehow, even in that state, you called me. We talked. Really talked. The kind of conversation that only happens when your guard is fully down and you're just being yourself with nowhere to be and nothing to perform. I loved every minute of it.

And then, somewhere between the talking and the silence, you asked me to stay on call while you slept.

It reminded me of Bangalore. Right after Mumbai. I was the one who was drunk that time, falling asleep with my phone in my hand — and it was just silence. Just our breathing, quietly existing in each other's ears. No words needed. No plans. Just the sound of someone being alive on the other end.

Those are the sleeps I love most. The ones we share virtually, with nothing between us except a call that neither of us hangs up.

In those moments, you feel like you're right next to me. And for a few hours, that's more than enough.

The day before yesterday we couldn't talk at all. I stretched my eyes watching YouTube until they gave up on me. Sleep didn't feel like rest. It just felt like the absence of you.

I want to be your alarm. And your sleeping therapy. Both. Daily. Non-negotiable.

The Ladoo Surprise

Today, 4th August 2026. 12pm.

I drove 2 kilometres to a shop called Sonalben Khakhrawala. I had a picture on my phone — khajoor gaund ladoo, exactly the kind you'd been craving — and I walked up and asked, "Is this available?"

They said yes. Why not.

I got three packs instead of two, because two felt like it wasn't enough for someone whose cravings deserve to be taken seriously.

I handed them over to Sunil bhai. He immediately asked, "What's the charge?"

I smiled. "I'll never charge anything from my close ones."

He pushed back. Said she'd yell at him. I told him — tell her to send me those KitKats she owes me. He looked genuinely confused.

I walked home feeling like I'd won something.

And no, I'm not telling you any of this. Someone in this story likes surprises more than they like knowing things in advance.

We had a conversation once that I didn't know how to hold.

Not a fight. Not a goodbye. Just two people sitting with the truth of something that had no clean answer.

We said it gently — that this probably needs to slow down. That there's no ending in sight, and maybe that's the problem. That feelings this real, sitting in a space this undefined, can quietly become heavy for both people if nobody says anything.

After The Last Page...

If you've reached here... thank you.

Not just for reading this book. But for living every page of it with me.

While making this, I realised that memories aren't made from grand gestures. They're made from tiny moments that quietly become part of your everyday life. A morning call. A random Snap. A silly joke. A three-hour conversation that felt like thirty minutes. Waiting for your notification without even realizing you were waiting.

You became a habit I never wanted to break.

This isn't an ending. It's just the first time we stopped to notice how beautiful the middle already is.

I've seen you from closer than most people ever will. I've seen the version that laughs until her stomach hurts, blushes for no reason, says "Su nathi" with the cutest smile, spins around on her chair, and somehow makes an ordinary day feel special just by being in it.

Thank you for trusting me with that version of you.

If one day you hear someone say "Ho ka," unwrap a KitKat, lose an earring, see a sunflower, eat terrible pizza, walk on a beach at midnight, or someone casually asks you, "Su kare che?"...

I hope you smile. Not just because of what this was — but because of what it still is.

You'll always be my 🐣🌻. And no matter how many chapters life writes after this one... this will always remain one of my favourites.
